Personal Concept · 2025

Wishlet

Role
UX Research
UI Design
Timeline
November 2025
Team
Personal Wishlet Lead
6 designers
Tools
Figma
Illustrator

Challenge

Young adults experiencing a gap in financial literacy

Trends, influencer picks and one-tap checkout make spending emotional and instant, while serious finance apps feel too intimidating to open.

Problem

Shopping lost its natural pause.

Every convenience removed a checkpoint. There's no longer a moment between seeing something and owning it.

2× Shoppers with a trolley instead of a basket buy more than double (Larsen et al., 2020).

How might we put a pause between seeing and buying, without killing the fun of shopping?

Solution

A wishlist instead of a cart.

Items wait in a list you revisit. Wishlet then helps you rethink, see the cost and get a second opinion.

Swipe to decide

Keep, remove or skip each saved item, like a dating app for your wishlist.

Your budget as a visual

The bar goes up the more you spend, capping at the limit you set for yourself at the start of every month.

Ask your friends

Friends vote keep or drop on your list, or lend you something they already own.

Outcome

Tested with 12 users, who found it calming, not stressful.

Testers grasped the concept instantly. Next, I'd fix two things: the browse page could nudge people to shop more, and playful visuals sometimes hid what was tappable.

Oh, it's basically Tinder for shopping. I got it instantly.
